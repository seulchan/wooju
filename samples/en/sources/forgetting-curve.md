# Forgetting curve

## One-line Summary

Memory for new material drops steeply within days unless it is revisited, and each revisit slows the next drop.

## Key Ideas

- Hermann Ebbinghaus measured his own recall of nonsense syllables in the 1880s and found that most forgetting happens soon after learning.
- The curve is steep at first and then flattens: what survives the first few days tends to last.
- Reviewing before the drop restores recall and makes the next decline slower.
- The shape depends on the material and the person; meaningful material is forgotten more slowly than nonsense syllables.

## Limitations / Caveats

- The original experiments had one subject, Ebbinghaus himself.
- The numbers often quoted ("most of it gone within a day") come from nonsense syllables, not from material you care about.
